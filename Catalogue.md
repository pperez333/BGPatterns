# A starter catalogue of board game mechanics

## 1. Turn structure and action systems

### 1. Action Points / Action Point Allowance
**Core idea:** Players get a limited number of actions each turn and choose how to spend them.

**Problem solved:** Gives flexibility without requiring complex turn rules.

**Decision space:** Optimising limited actions.

**Examples:** *Pandemic*, *Gloomhaven* (in a looser sense), *Mage Knight*.

**Strengths:** High agency, clean structure.  
**Weaknesses:** Can lead to analysis paralysis.

---

### 2. Worker Placement
**Core idea:** Players place workers on action spaces to claim actions, often blocking others.

**Problem solved:** Creates competition over scarce actions.

**Decision space:** Timing, blocking, prioritisation.

**Examples:** *Agricola*, *Lords of Waterdeep*, *Viticulture*.

**Strengths:** Strong interaction, intuitive.  
**Weaknesses:** Can feel punitive if too tight.

---

### 3. Action Selection
**Core idea:** Players choose one action from a menu, often affecting what others may do.

**Problem solved:** Provides strategic turn identity and interaction.

**Examples:** *Puerto Rico*, *Race for the Galaxy*.

**Strengths:** Elegant, often highly interactive.  
**Weaknesses:** Can become scripted if balance is weak.

---

### 4. Programming
**Core idea:** Players plan actions in advance, then resolve them later.

**Problem solved:** Introduces prediction, chaos, and indirect interaction.

**Examples:** *RoboRally*, *Mechs vs. Minions*.

**Strengths:** Tense and funny, rewards foresight.  
**Weaknesses:** Can feel frustrating if outcomes are too chaotic.

---

### 5. Simultaneous Action Selection
**Core idea:** Everyone chooses actions at the same time, then reveals.

**Problem solved:** Reduces downtime and increases mind games.

**Examples:** *7 Wonders*, *Diplomacy*.

**Strengths:** Fast, interactive.  
**Weaknesses:** Can be opaque for new players.

---

### 6. Variable Turn Order
**Core idea:** Turn order changes dynamically based on choices, bidding, track position, etc.

**Problem solved:** Makes timing itself strategically meaningful.

**Examples:** *Power Grid*, *Patchwork*.

**Strengths:** Rich tactical depth.  
**Weaknesses:** Can be unintuitive.

---

## 2. Resource and economic systems

### 7. Resource Management
**Core idea:** Players collect, convert, spend, and optimise resources.

**Problem solved:** Creates long-term planning and trade-offs.

**Examples:** *Catan*, *Brass: Birmingham*.

**Strengths:** Universally useful, supports many genres.  
**Weaknesses:** Can become spreadsheet-like.

---

### 8. Engine Building
**Core idea:** Players build systems that generate increasing value over time.

**Problem solved:** Creates growth arcs and strategic satisfaction.

**Examples:** *Terraforming Mars*, *Wingspan*, *Splendor*.

**Strengths:** Very rewarding, strong progression.  
**Weaknesses:** Runaway leader risk.

---

### 9. Tableau Building
**Core idea:** Players build a personal area of cards/tokens with persistent effects.

**Problem solved:** Gives visible progress and combinatorial play.

**Examples:** *Race for the Galaxy*, *Res Arcana*.

**Strengths:** Supports variety and synergies.  
**Weaknesses:** Can produce difficult board-state parsing.

---

### 10. Set Collection
**Core idea:** Players gather matching or complementary groups for value.

**Problem solved:** Creates clear goals and drafting/trading tension.

**Examples:** *Ticket to Ride*, *Sushi Go!*

**Strengths:** Easy to teach.  
**Weaknesses:** Can feel shallow unless combined with other systems.

---

### 11. Auction / Bidding
**Core idea:** Players compete by offering resources, money, or position for assets.

**Problem solved:** Lets players determine value dynamically.

**Examples:** *Modern Art*, *Ra*, *Power Grid*.

**Strengths:** Highly interactive, psychologically rich.  
**Weaknesses:** Weak with inexperienced groups if valuation is unclear.

---

### 12. Trading / Negotiation
**Core idea:** Players exchange resources, promises, or favours.

**Problem solved:** Creates social dynamics and emergent value.

**Examples:** *Catan*, *Chinatown*, *Bohnanza*.

**Strengths:** Memorable player interaction.  
**Weaknesses:** Group-dependent, can favour extroverts.

---

## 3. Card and deck systems

### 13. Deck Building
**Core idea:** Players start with a basic deck and improve it during play.

**Problem solved:** Combines progression with probabilistic planning.

**Examples:** *Dominion*, *Clank!*, *Star Realms*.

**Strengths:** Strong sense of growth.  
**Weaknesses:** Shuffle/randomness can undermine plans.

---

### 14. Deck Construction
**Core idea:** Players build a deck before play from a broader pool.

**Problem solved:** Supports metagame, customisation, identity.

**Examples:** *Magic: The Gathering*, *Android: Netrunner*.

**Strengths:** Enormous depth.  
**Weaknesses:** Barrier to entry, collection imbalance.

---

### 15. Hand Management
**Core idea:** Players must use a limited hand efficiently, often with timing constraints.

**Problem solved:** Creates tactical planning and opportunity cost.

**Examples:** *Hanabi*, *Terraforming Mars*, *Brass* card systems.

**Strengths:** Broadly applicable.  
**Weaknesses:** Can cause AP.

---

### 16. Drafting
**Core idea:** Players choose from a temporary selection and pass the rest on.

**Problem solved:** Creates constrained choice and indirect interaction.

**Examples:** *7 Wonders*, *Sushi Go!*, *Blood Rage*.

**Strengths:** Fast, elegant, interactive.  
**Weaknesses:** Harder to evaluate choices for beginners.

---

## 4. Spatial and map-based systems

### 17. Area Control / Area Majority
**Core idea:** Players compete for dominance in regions.

**Problem solved:** Gives geography strategic importance.

**Examples:** *Risk*, *El Grande*, *Blood Rage*.

**Strengths:** Strong conflict and map tension.  
**Weaknesses:** Can encourage kingmaking or gang-ups.

---

### 18. Tile Placement
**Core idea:** Players place tiles to build the board or score patterns.

**Problem solved:** Creates spatial puzzles and evolving state.

**Examples:** *Carcassonne*, *Cascadia*.

**Strengths:** Visual and intuitive.  
**Weaknesses:** Can become multiplayer solitaire if interaction is low.

---

### 19. Route Building / Network Building
**Core idea:** Players create connected paths or networks on the board.

**Problem solved:** Makes expansion and positioning meaningful.

**Examples:** *Ticket to Ride*, *Brass*, *Age of Steam*.

**Strengths:** Strong tension over shared space.  
**Weaknesses:** Can punish mistakes harshly.

---

### 20. Grid Movement / Tactical Positioning
**Core idea:** Unit position on a map or grid affects options and outcomes.

**Problem solved:** Gives combat or movement concrete, spatial depth.

**Examples:** *Chess*, *Gloomhaven*, *Summoner Wars*.

**Strengths:** Deep tactics.  
**Weaknesses:** Can be rules-heavy.

---

### 21. Pattern Building
**Core idea:** Players arrange pieces to satisfy adjacency, shape, or scoring constraints.

**Problem solved:** Creates spatial optimisation.

**Examples:** *Azul*, *Sagrada*.

**Strengths:** Accessible and satisfying.  
**Weaknesses:** Often lower direct interaction.

---

## 5. Conflict, uncertainty, and risk

### 22. Dice Rolling / Random Resolution
**Core idea:** Outcomes are determined partly by dice or equivalent randomness.

**Problem solved:** Adds uncertainty, drama, and accessibility.

**Examples:** *Yahtzee*, *Risk*, *King of Tokyo*.

**Strengths:** Exciting, easy to understand.  
**Weaknesses:** Can feel unfair.

---

### 23. Push Your Luck
**Core idea:** Players may continue for greater reward while risking loss.

**Problem solved:** Produces tension from voluntary risk.

**Examples:** *Can’t Stop*, *Incan Gold*, *Quacks of Quedlinburg*.

**Strengths:** Highly engaging and emotional.  
**Weaknesses:** Can be repetitive if not enough strategic context.

---

### 24. Bluffing
**Core idea:** Players misrepresent intentions, hidden information, or confidence.

**Problem solved:** Creates psychological interaction.

**Examples:** *Skull*, *Coup*.

**Strengths:** Socially rich, dramatic.  
**Weaknesses:** Group-dependent; not all players enjoy deception.

---

### 25. Hidden Roles
**Core idea:** Players have secret teams or objectives.

**Problem solved:** Introduces deduction, suspicion, and social tension.

**Examples:** *The Resistance*, *Secret Hitler*, *Werewolf*.

**Strengths:** Very memorable.  
**Weaknesses:** Player elimination or social discomfort can be issues.

---

### 26. Deduction
**Core idea:** Players infer hidden information from clues and constrained possibilities.

**Problem solved:** Creates intellectual challenge and discovery.

**Examples:** *Cluedo*, *Cryptid*.

**Strengths:** Strong “aha” moments.  
**Weaknesses:** Can stall if clues are poorly paced.

---

### 27. Combat Resolution
**Core idea:** Specific procedures determine battle outcomes, often using stats, cards, or dice.

**Problem solved:** Formalises conflict.

**Examples:** Nearly all conflict games; *Kemet*, *Root*, *Memoir ’44*.

**Strengths:** Can be exciting and thematic.  
**Weaknesses:** Overly detailed systems slow the game.

---

## 6. Information and asymmetry

### 28. Hidden Information
**Core idea:** Some game state is private.

**Problem solved:** Enables surprise, deduction, bluffing.

**Examples:** Most card games; *Stratego*.

**Strengths:** Rich mind games.  
**Weaknesses:** Can create feel-bad moments if too swingy.

---

### 29. Asymmetric Powers
**Core idea:** Different players have distinct rules, capabilities, or victory incentives.

**Problem solved:** Increases replayability and varied strategy.

**Examples:** *Root*, *Terra Mystica*, *Cosmic Encounter*.

**Strengths:** Strong identity and depth.  
**Weaknesses:** Very difficult to balance and teach.

---

### 30. Variable Player Powers
**Core idea:** Players begin with a special power, but still share a common system.

**Problem solved:** Adds variety without full asymmetry.

**Examples:** *Small World*, *Puerto Rico* role effects in a softer sense.

**Strengths:** Easier than full asymmetry.  
**Weaknesses:** Some powers may dominate.

---

## 7. Scoring and incentives

### 31. Victory Point Salad
**Core idea:** Players can score from many different sources.

**Problem solved:** Supports multiple viable strategies and flexible planning.

**Examples:** *Castles of Burgundy*, *Terraforming Mars*.

**Strengths:** Encourages adaptation.  
**Weaknesses:** Can obscure what is actually important.

---

### 32. Contracts / Public Objectives / Private Objectives
**Core idea:** Players pursue explicit tasks for rewards.

**Problem solved:** Provides short- and long-term direction.

**Examples:** *Ticket to Ride* (private routes), *Lords of Waterdeep*.

**Strengths:** Focuses play.  
**Weaknesses:** Bad objective balance can distort the game.

---

### 33. End-Game Trigger / Race
**Core idea:** Players can influence when the game ends.

**Problem solved:** Makes pacing strategic.

**Examples:** *Terraforming Mars*, *Splendor*.

**Strengths:** Adds timing tension.  
**Weaknesses:** Novices may miss tempo implications.

---

## 8. Cooperative and semi-cooperative structures

### 34. Co-operative Play
**Core idea:** Players win or lose together against the system.

**Problem solved:** Encourages joint planning and shared tension.

**Examples:** *Pandemic*, *Spirit Island*.

**Strengths:** Inclusive, team-oriented.  
**Weaknesses:** Quarterbacking problem.

---

### 35. Traitor / Semi-Co-operative
**Core idea:** Players appear aligned, but some may betray or have conflicting incentives.

**Problem solved:** Mixes co-operation with suspicion.

**Examples:** *Battlestar Galactica*, *Dead of Winter*.

**Strengths:** High drama.  
**Weaknesses:** Can be messy or unfair.

---

### 36. Communication Limits
**Core idea:** Players may not fully share information.

**Problem solved:** Prevents co-op optimisation and preserves uncertainty.

**Examples:** *Hanabi*, *The Crew*.

**Strengths:** Elegant solution to quarterbacking.  
**Weaknesses:** Can frustrate some players.

---

## 9. Progression and campaign structures

### 37. Legacy
**Core idea:** Game state changes permanently across sessions.

**Problem solved:** Creates long-term narrative and consequence.

**Examples:** *Pandemic Legacy*, *Charterstone*.

**Strengths:** Very immersive.  
**Weaknesses:** Finite and difficult to replay.

---

### 38. Campaign Progression
**Core idea:** Sessions connect through unlocking content, growth, or story.

**Problem solved:** Sustains engagement over time.

**Examples:** *Gloomhaven*, *Sleeping Gods*.

**Strengths:** Strong investment.  
**Weaknesses:** Scheduling and commitment burden.

---

### 39. Tech Tree / Upgrade Tracks
**Core idea:** Players unlock stronger or more specialised capabilities over time.

**Problem solved:** Structures development choices.

**Examples:** *Civilisation*-style games, *Scythe* upgrade elements.

**Strengths:** Clear progression.  
**Weaknesses:** Can become deterministic.

---

# Mechanics that are often better treated as “meta-patterns”

Some concepts are not exactly mechanics themselves, but are recurring design structures:

### 40. Catch-Up Mechanisms
Helps trailing players remain competitive.

### 41. Runaway Leader Prevention
Prevents early advantage from snowballing too hard.

### 42. Player Elimination
Removed players stop participating before the end.

### 43. Kingmaking
A losing player can decide which other player wins.

### 44. Downtime Mitigation
Structural techniques to keep players engaged out of turn.

### 45. Emergent Narrative
Systems create memorable stories without scripted events.

These are closer to **design concerns** or **cross-cutting patterns**, analogous to architectural concerns in software.

# A pattern-entry example

Here’s how one might look in full.

## Worker Placement

**Intent:**  
Create competition over action opportunities by making actions scarce and exclusive.

**Also known as:**  
Placement blocking, action-space occupation.

**Context:**  
Useful when you want player interaction without direct combat, and when turn-level decisions should feel concrete and contested.

**Problem:**  
How do you let players choose actions freely while ensuring other players’ choices matter?

**Forces:**  
- Players need meaningful agency  
- Interaction should be legible  
- Blocking should matter but not feel hopeless  
- Turn options must remain understandable

**Solution:**  
Provide a shared board of action spaces. Each player has a limited number of workers to place. Occupied spaces become unavailable, more expensive, or otherwise altered for others.

**Consequences:**  
- Strong interaction through blocking  
- Tension over turn order and timing  
- Natural pacing through worker scarcity  
- Risk of frustration if critical actions are too limited

**Common variants:**  
- Exclusive spaces  
- Spaces with stacking but increasing cost  
- Retrieval phase before reuse  
- Different worker types unlocking different actions

**Examples:**  
*Agricola*, *Viticulture*, *Stone Age*.

**Pairs well with:**  
Resource management, engine building, seasonal structure, variable player powers.

**Anti-patterns / failure modes:**  
- One “must-have” space every round  
- Too many equivalent spaces, reducing tension  
- Excessive punishment for going later in turn order
